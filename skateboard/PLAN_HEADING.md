# Heading plan: make the ride hold its line (steer v13)

Written 2026-11-26. Sim only until a policy passes; nothing here runs on the
robot.

**Goal:** with the stick centred, the board keeps the heading it started on,
so a 25 s ride ends near its line instead of tens of cm to one side.

**Scope:** `Mjlab-SkateSplit-Steer-Pupper-v3` rewards and the sim tools.
Balance, the split ride task and the centred tasks are untouched. The
robot-side heading hold and the exported file's `heading_hold` block do not
change.

Naming: v11 was the extra carpet run, and a teammate's `board_progress` run
is called v12, so this one is v13.

## What the investigation found (steer v10, 16 boards, 0.3 m/s, x3 drag, 25 s)

How the hold works (same formula in sim and on the robot):

- With the stick under 0.1 and the board moving, the yaw command the policy
  sees is `clip(kp * (target - robot heading), ±clip)` with kp 1.0 and clip
  0.1 (`src/mjlab/tasks/velocity/mdp/velocity_command.py:234-248`). The sim
  captures the board's heading as the target
  (`src/mjlab/tasks/pupper_skate/mdp/commands.py:32`); the robot captures its
  own IMU yaw. There is no deadband.
- A heading error of 3 / 5 / 7 degrees gives a command of 0.052 / 0.087 / 0.1.

Why the heading settles several degrees off:

- The policy turns the board only about 0.185 rad/s per unit of that command
  (0.06 to 0.34 across boards; open-loop probe: the hold's command forced to
  +0.08 and -0.08 for 5 s each, board yaw rate averaged in each window), so
  the hold has at most about 0.019 rad/s of authority.
- With a zero command the policy still turns the board by itself: -0.032 to
  +0.019 rad/s across boards (mean -0.008, mean size 0.015). The heading
  settles where the weak correction balances that pull.
- The board's heading also sits about 1 degree to the right of the robot's
  (mean size 1.17 degrees, the same sign in 15 of 16 boards), which the hold
  cannot see. That the robot stands rotated on the board is the reading of
  that measurement, not a separate one.
- Sideways drift is explained by board heading alone (predicted per board to
  within 0.8 cm).
- Why the policy follows corrections weakly: ignoring a 0.05 correction costs
  only 5% of `track_yaw_rate`, and `board_yaw_rate` and `board_alignment` are
  flat at 1 to 2 degrees.

Retuning the hold alone (no retraining), drift after 25 s:

| Hold | Range (cm) | Mean size (cm) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kp 1, clip 0.1 (shipped) | -56 to +85 | 29.0 |
| kp 4, clip 0.1 | -53 to +65 | 22.9 |
| kp 4, clip 0.3 | -51 to +34 | 20.4 |
| kp 4, ki 1, clip 0.1 | -72 to +67 | 24.8 |
| kp 4, ki 1, clip 0.3 | -41 to +19 | 14.3 |

The best retune halves the drift but needs a robot-side code change, pushes
the command above 0.1 for half the ride (which the policy reads as a carve),
and depends on IMU yaw drift that is not measured. So this plan trains the
policy to answer the existing hold instead, and leaves the hold alone.

Not determined: the pull and IMU drift on hardware; why the pull is biased to
the right; anything beyond one seed, x3 drag and 0.3 m/s.

## Design

Two things make the hold work with its shipped gain and clip:

1. **The board should not turn by itself, and should turn when the hold asks.**
   Outside carves, pay the board's yaw rate for matching
   `HOLD_BOARD_GAIN * (yaw command)`. At zero command that target is zero
   (no pull); at the hold's largest command, 0.1, it is 0.03 rad/s, which the
   board can do (a medium carve already asks 0.03). With that response the
   loop's authority is 0.03 rad/s against today's 0.019, and the pull it has
   to beat is gone.
2. **The robot should stand square on the board.** Tighten the yaw part of
   `board_alignment`.

An earlier run (v8) paid for zero board yaw rate outside carves together with
a sideways-creep charge and failed its bar; the stick-0 pull did go away.
This plan uses the yaw-rate term without the creep charge, and scales its
target with the hold's command.

## What is left after v13 (measured 2026-11-27)

Same 25 s ride, with the board's and the robot's headings recorded
separately (time means over 2 to 25 s per board, 16 boards; "mean size" is
the mean over boards of the size of each board's time mean):

| | v10 | v13 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Robot heading error against the hold's target, mean size | 1.36 deg | 0.29 deg |
| Board heading against the robot's, mean | -1.16 deg (mean size 1.17; negative on 15 of 16 boards, -2.82 to +0.02) | -1.49 deg (negative on all 16 boards, -0.18 to -3.58) |
| Board heading error (from its heading at the start), mean size | 2.34 deg | 1.38 deg |
| Board yaw rate per unit of hold command, closed loop | 0.008 | 0.198 (per board 0.09 to 0.88) |
| Hold command at the clip | 5% of steps | 0.5% of steps |

How each line was obtained: the first three are time means of the recorded
headings over 2 to 25 s. The yaw-rate line is one least-squares fit, pooled
over boards, of the board's yaw rate on the hold's command, both smoothed
over 1 s, from 2 s on, during the ordinary ride (the hold closing its own
loop). It is not the open-loop probe behind v10's 0.185 above (forced
+0.08 and -0.08 commands), so 0.008 and 0.185 are different measurements of
v10, not a contradiction. The last line is the share of steps with the
command's size at 0.0999 or more.

- The robot now keeps its heading to 0.3 degrees of the hold's target. In
  the closed-loop fit the board's yaw rate moves with the hold's command far
  more than v10's does (0.198 against 0.008 per unit), with a wide spread
  between boards.
- The board's heading error (1.38 degrees) is close to the board's heading
  against the robot's (-1.49 degrees), since the robot's own error is small.
  Per-board drift is predicted by the board's heading to within 0.3 cm. The
  hold measures the robot's heading, so it cannot see the board's. Reading
  this as the board standing rotated about 1.5 degrees to the right of the
  robot, and that rotation as the cause of the drift, is an inference; v14
  (below) did not confirm it.
- `board_alignment` at yaw std 0.04 pays 0.65 at 1.5 degrees, and its episode
  value ended at 0.67. Why the policy keeps that rotation was not measured.

## Task 4: pay the board for holding the hold's heading (steer v14)

The hold's target in sim is the board's heading when the hold engaged. Pay
the board directly for staying on it, so the policy can either stand square
or carry its robot heading a little off to keep the board on line. On the
robot the target is the robot's own yaw at capture; with the robot placed
square to the board the two are the same.

Files: `src/mjlab/tasks/velocity/mdp/velocity_command.py` (a read-only
accessor only), `src/mjlab/tasks/pupper_skate/mdp/rewards.py`,
`mdp/__init__.py`, `src/mjlab/tasks/pupper_skate/pupper_skate_split_env_cfg.py`,
`skateboard/tools/make_notebook.py`, `notebooks/CS123_Pupper_Skate.ipynb`
(regenerated with the tool), `tests/test_pupper_skate_split.py`,
`tests/test_skate_notebook.py`.

1. The velocity command term gets a small read-only way to get, per env,
   whether the heading hold is active this step and its target heading. It
   must not change what the hold computes or emits.
2. New reward `board_heading_hold(env, command_name, std, min_speed,
   board_cfg)`: in envs where the hold is active and the forward speed
   command is at least `min_speed`, `exp(-(wrap(board heading - hold target)
   / std) ** 2)`; 0 in every other env (carves, standing, hold not active).
3. Steer config only: `r["board_heading_hold"]`, weight
   `BOARD_HEADING_HOLD_WEIGHT = 1.0`, `std = BOARD_HEADING_HOLD_STD = 0.03`
   (1.7 degrees; the 1.5 degree rotation then pays 0.47), `min_speed =
   STEER_YAW_MIN_SPEED`. A comment at the constants with the numbers from the
   table above. The steer docstring's list of what is paid is updated.
   Everything v13 set stays: `HOLD_BOARD_GAIN = 0.3`,
   `STEER_BOARD_ALIGN_STD_YAW = 0.04`, `board_side_creep` 0.0,
   `deck_feet_slide` -2.0.
4. `make_notebook.py`'s steer reward table carries `board_heading_hold` at
   1.0, and the notebook is regenerated with the tool. A term missing from
   that table can train at the wrong weight.
5. Tests first, seen to fail:
   - the accessor returns the same active mask and target the hold used, and
     the command the term emits is unchanged for a fixed seed;
   - `board_heading_hold`: a hold-active env at speed with the board on the
     target gets 1.0; 0.03 rad off gets `exp(-1)` to 3 decimals; an error
     across the ±pi wrap is treated as small; a carve env, a standing env and
     an env under `min_speed` get 0;
   - the steer config carries the term with weight 1.0 and std 0.03; the
     split ride, both balance tasks and the centred ride do not have it and
     build the rewards they build today;
   - the notebook table has `board_heading_hold` at 1.0 for the steer task
     only, and the regenerated notebook matches the tool's output.
6. No change to the hold's formula, gain, clip or thresholds, to
   observations, actions or terminations, or to the exported file's
   `heading_hold` block.

What the code shows (not a measured cause):

- The sim hold captures its target from the board's heading
  (`BoardHeadingHoldCommand._heading_hold_reference`) but measures its error
  from the robot's heading (`wrap_to_pi(target - robot heading)` in
  `UniformVelocityCommand.compute`). With the board rotated a fixed angle
  against the robot, the board can sit on the target only while the robot is
  off it by that angle, so only while the hold's command is not zero.
- In that state v13's `track_board_yaw_rate` term (weight 2.0, std 0.02)
  asks the board to turn at 0.3 times that command, so it charges a board
  that holds still there. At a 1.5 degree rotation (command 0.026) the charge
  is about 0.29 against the 0.53 that `board_heading_hold` (weight 1.0, std
  0.03) adds for putting the board on target, so the net is roughly half.
- The actor does not observe the board's pose: `board_pose` and the other
  board terms are critic-only observations. It can learn a constant bias,
  not a per-board correction.
- In training the hold recaptures its target every time it re-engages
  (after a carve, a stop or an episode reset), commands resample every 3 to
  8 s and episodes last 20 s. The drift test pins the command (0.3 m/s,
  stick 0), so it is one 25 s hold with one capture.
- Which of these, if any, explains the result is not established, nor why
  the stance is rotated.
